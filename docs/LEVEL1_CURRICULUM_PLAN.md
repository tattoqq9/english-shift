# English Shift — Level 1 Curriculum & Game Design Plan
Version: Draft 0.1
Date: 2026-09-02

## 0. Purpose

This document defines the **Level 1 full-game curriculum** before adding more code or content.

Level 1 is not a tutorial. It is the first complete pass through English Shift:

- Player reads/listens to the situation.
- Player chooses an appropriate English response.
- The response affects the customer, information state, score, trust, and outcome.
- By the end of Level 1, the player has encountered the major grammar structures needed for practical junior-high to high-school-level English in meaningful contexts.
- Level 2 and Level 3 reuse the same curriculum with less support:
  - Level 1: SELECT
  - Level 2: BUILD
  - Level 3: RECALL with controlled vocabulary

The immediate development priority is to **complete and balance Level 1 first**.

---

# 1. Overall Size

## Proposed first target

**8 Chapters × 6 Shifts = 48 Shifts**

Estimated play time:

- 1 Shift: 10–15 minutes
- Total Level 1: roughly 8–12 hours

A Shift is a game day, not a real-world calendar day.

Typical Shift:

- 5–7 normal customer encounters
- 0–1 short random event
- 0–1 major/special customer or incident
- Day Result
- 1–3 new language targets at most
- Previously learned targets mixed in

## Content ratio

Early game:

- New: 60%
- Review: 40%

Middle game:

- New: 40%
- Review: 60%

Late game:

- New: 20–30%
- Review/integration: 70–80%

Chapter 8 should contain almost no major new grammar.

# 2. Curriculum Principles

## 2.1 Store first, grammar second

Do not design:

> Today = Present Perfect Lesson

Design:

> Customer returns a product and the player needs to discover what they have already tried.

Then assign:

- Present perfect
- Past tense
- Troubleshooting vocabulary

The visible goal is always a **job/game goal**.

---

## 2.2 One grammar point must appear in multiple stores

Bad:

- Comparative = Clothing only
- Present perfect = Electronics only

Good:

Comparative:
- Clothing: lighter / cheaper
- Sports: more durable
- Electronics: faster
- Restaurant: spicier
- Hotel: quieter

Present perfect:
- Sports: Have you used this before?
- Electronics: Have you tried restarting it?
- Hotel: Have you stayed with us before?
- Returns: Have you opened the package?

The player learns a transferable language pattern rather than memorizing a scene.

---

## 2.3 Separate production and recognition targets

Some low-frequency advanced structures do not need to become free-speaking targets immediately.

Target types:

- **P = Productive:** player should eventually use it in Level 2/3.
- **R = Recognition:** player should understand it in customer speech, staff messages, notices, reports, etc.
- **P/R = both**

This prevents unnatural situations created only to force rare grammar into conversation.

# 6. Spaced Review Rule

Every newly introduced high-value structure should ideally reappear:

1. Same Shift
2. Next Shift
3. Approximately +3 Shifts
4. Approximately +7 Shifts, preferably in another store
5. Approximately +14 Shifts, preferably in an event
6. Final Chapter

Example:

Present perfect:
- Day 13: first introduction
- Day 14: same store review
- Day 16: outdoor experience
- Day 21: electronics troubleshooting
- Day 28: restaurant experience/allergy history
- Day 31+: hotel history
- Day 47–48: final integration

This recurrence pattern should later be encoded in content planning tools rather than handled manually.

# 7. Game Variety Matrix

Level 1 should not consist of 48 recommendation shifts.

Core activity types:

| Type | Player objective | Strong chapters |
|---|---|---|
| Recommendation | discover needs → select product | 2,3,4,7 |
| Information Hunt | ask efficient questions → identify target | 1,4,6 |
| Checkout / Processing | accurately complete transaction | 1,5 |
| Troubleshooting | identify cause → choose next action | 3,4,6 |
| Staff Coordination | relay customer information | 4,6,8 |
| Policy Judgment | understand rules → decide fairly | 6,7 |
| Incident Investigation | collect evidence → infer sequence/cause | 6,7,8 |
| Rush | maximize successful service under light time pressure | 1,5,8 |
| Repeat Customer | use prior customer history | 3 onward |
| VIP / High Stakes | register + correct judgment | 7,8 |
| Multi-customer | prioritize competing requests | 8 |

Suggested distribution across 48 Shifts:

- Recommendation-heavy: 12
- Information/processing: 8
- Troubleshooting: 6
- Staff coordination: 5
- Incident/policy: 6
- Rush: 4
- Mixed / chapter checks / final: 7

Exact numbers can change after playtesting.

# 8. Random / Micro Events

Major events should not be the only source of variety.

Low-cost micro-events:

- Card payment error
- Incorrect price tag
- Last item in stock
- Customer answers a phone call
- Another customer interrupts
- Staff member is on break
- Closing time approaching
- Customer changes budget
- Product demo battery is empty
- Queue suddenly grows
- Receipt printer fails
- Customer returns later the same day

Micro-events reuse existing language but change the decision context.

---

# 9. Time and Pressure

Do not make early learning dependent on fast reactions.

Recommended:

Normal campaign:
- No hard timer
- Patience and efficiency provide soft pressure

Challenge/Rush Shift:
- Shift timer
- actions consume time
- bonus for throughput and accuracy

Example future economy:

- Ask question: -20 sec
- Check stock: -40 sec
- Call specialist: -60 sec
- Good recommendation: sale + trust
- Bad upsell: revenue may rise but trust/store rating falls

This creates strategy without making beginners panic.

---

# 10. Repeat Customers

Repeat customers are high-value because they provide:

- narrative continuity
- asset reuse
- memory/recognition gameplay
- natural review of old grammar

Example:

Day 3 — Mia:
- buys running earphones

Day 17 — Mia:
- reports how they have been working
- naturally introduces present perfect

Day 35 — Mia:
- buys a gift for her brother
- old information is relevant but not sufficient

This should become a formal content system.

# 11. Level 2 / Level 3 Placement

Do not interrupt Level 1 campaign development now.

Future structure:

## After each chapter

Example after Day 6:

> Convenience Store — Level 1 Clear  
> BUILD SHIFT unlocked

Optional Level 2 review uses only the grammar/vocabulary from that chapter.

After Day 12:
- Clothing BUILD Shift unlocked

...etc.

## After full Level 1 completion

Unlock:
- Full Level 2 second lap
- or Adaptive difficulty mode

Later:
- Level 3 RECALL for mastered language

Possible modes:

- STANDARD: Level 1 dominant
- CHALLENGE: more Level 2
- EXPERT: mastered phrases become Level 3

Do not implement these until Level 1 content loop is validated.

# 12. Controlled Vocabulary Scope

Level 1 does **not** need to reproduce the entire vocabulary count of Japanese school education.

English Shift is domain-focused.

Initial design target:

- 450–600 productive core lemmas
- additional recognition-only store/product vocabulary
- grammar coverage broader than vocabulary coverage
- store-specific vocabulary unlocked chapter by chapter

Vocabulary categories:

1. General verbs
2. Question words
3. Service/politeness
4. Money/quantity
5. Product properties
6. Clothing
7. Sports/outdoor
8. Electronics
9. Food/restaurant
10. Hotel/travel
11. Returns/problems
12. Staff/incident language

This limited vocabulary later makes Level 3 Controlled English technically feasible.

# 14. Level 1 Completion Criteria

Before moving serious development effort to Level 2/3, Level 1 should satisfy:

## Learning

- Major language units have meaningful first-use contexts.
- High-value language reappears across multiple stores.
- No chapter introduces too many structures at once.
- Final shifts actually reuse earlier language.

## Game

- At least 5 core activity types feel meaningfully different.
- Recommendation is not more than roughly one third of the full experience.
- Events alter decisions, not just dialogue flavor.
- Repeat customers create continuity.
- Trust / efficiency / customer fit lead to meaningful tradeoffs.

## UX

- Comfortable on Android/iPhone portrait screens.
- Customer reactions clearly communicate outcomes.
- Long conversations remain easy to follow.
- A 10–15 minute Shift feels complete.
